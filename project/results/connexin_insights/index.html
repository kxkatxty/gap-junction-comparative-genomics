Main point

Connexins are a vertebrate family with conserved subfamilies (α GJA, β GJB, γ GJC, δ GJD), not a Drosophila-style name-chaos problem. α and β co-occur across tetrapods and fish. Proteins stay closer than innexins (~57% within-subfamily vs ~40% between; α↔β ~46%). Gene architecture is neighborhood-conserved: GJA4 lives in the β-cluster, GJA1 occupies a separate locus — two genomic addresses, not one connexin pile.

Gene structure

Two loci: GJA4 in a β-cluster, GJA1 on its own

Unlike Drosophila innexins, where genomic clusters cut across the tree, vertebrate connexins keep a stable neighborhood around the β-cluster (GJA4 with GJB3–GJB5). Physically adjacent paralogs such as cx38 / gjb3 / gjb4 / gja4 are a classic signature of tandem gene duplication by local unequal crossing-over — how much of the vertebrate connexin repertoire expanded into tight genomic arrays — whereas whole-genome duplications also scattered other paralogs onto separate chromosomes (notably the isolated GJA1 address). Xenopus already has both loci. Between Xenopus laevis (allotetraploid L-homeolog) and diploid X. tropicalis, the GJA4 microsynteny plot shows a local chromosomal inversion: in X. laevis the flank kdm1a.L → tmem30b.L → zmpste24.L → smim12.L sits to the left of gja4.L; in X. tropicalis that same conserved block is inverted and sits to the right of gja4. The neighbourhood is retained; only the orientation relative to the connexin locus changed after tetraploidization / diploidization.

GJA4 cross-species microsynteny — tandem β-cluster (GJB3–GJB5, DLGAP3, SMIM12); Xenopus L vs tropicalis inversion of the kdm1a–smim12 block relative to gja4.

Note: Connexin evolution mixes tandem clusters (local unequal crossing-over) with WGD-dispersed addresses (GJA1). Compare GJA4 with GJA1. Reading Xenopus at gene-order resolution reveals post-duplication inversion dynamics — not just “shared flanks”.

On the GJA1 microsynteny plot, X. laevis gja1.L can look isolated while Danio has a full neighbourhood. That blank space is usually a short scaffold or assembly gap in the draft genome — not a biological gene island.

Gene & protein architecture

Lengths stay connexin-like; exons stay simple

Reference connexins are typically ~250–380 aa with few exons (panel median 2). That compactness matches the textbook rule: connexin coding regions usually lack introns (ORF in one exon; often a separate 5′ UTR exon). Innexins, by contrast, keep coding-region introns and can splice multiple isoforms from one locus.

Protein length by vertebrate clade.

Note: Mammals ~333 aa, birds ~375 aa, amphibians ~308 aa, fishes shorter (~256–277 aa). Length is conserved enough to stay “connexin-shaped”.

Mean exon count for reference connexins by clade.

Note: Compact, usually intron-free coding architecture is a clade-wide connexin character — another reason they are not remodelled copies of intron-rich innexins. δ GJD can spike toward ~22 exons in clade×subfamily plots — usually a GFF fusion with intron-rich flanks (e.g. DLGAP3/SMIM12), not a real 22-exon connexin.

Summary

Connexins diversified inside vertebrates as named α/β/γ/δ classes that stay sequence-close and synteny-stable (GJA4 β-cluster vs a separate GJA1 locus). Innexins, by contrast, diversified by clade-specific duplication with misleading gene names and little conserved cross-phylum synteny.
